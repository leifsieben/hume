\section{Supplementary Methods}

Implementation details are given where they are not recoverable from the source: the reasons for
a choice, the measurements behind it, and the traps that produced silently wrong numbers.

\subsection{Descriptor selection}

RDKit's 217 descriptors and Mordred's 1{,}613 two-dimensional descriptors give a union of
1{,}830 columns; the 3D block needs conformers and is excluded throughout. Over 20{,}000 ChEMBL
molecules we drop columns that are constant or less than 95\% finite (452 sparse, 104 constant;
1{,}275 remain), rank-transform the rest so that redundancy is detected up to any monotone
reparametrisation rather than only linear ones, and keep a descriptor unless it correlates
$|\rho| \geq 0.99$ with one already kept. Candidates are visited cheapest-first, so the survivor
of each redundant group is its cheapest member; Mordred descriptors are charged their family
cost divided by family size, since that is what one costs once its family's shared intermediate
exists. The threshold is a real choice rather than a plateau: 1{,}197 columns survive at
$|\rho| = 1.0$, 865 at $0.99$, and 632 at $0.95$.

Fifteen Mordred families lose every column. The largest casualty is the matrix-spectral block:
all 121 \texttt{SpMax}/\texttt{SpDiam}/\texttt{SpAD}/\texttt{SpMAD}/\texttt{SpAbs}/\texttt{VE*}/\texttt{VR*}
scalars over the adjacency, Barysz, distance and detour matrices have zero survivors. Because
the cover runs cheapest-first, each had a \emph{cheaper} non-spectral correlate; collapsing an
$n \times n$ matrix to one spectral scalar discards whatever was new about the matrix.
Autocorrelation contributes 419 of the surviving 865 and E-state 50.

\subsection{Fingerprint}

On 3{,}000 molecules averaging 28.9 heavy atoms, $r = 3$ yields 66.4 distinct environments per
molecule against 64.8 on-bits --- a 2.4\% loss to hash collisions, versus 1.8\% at $r = 2$.
Widening to 4096 bits reduces the $r = 3$ loss to 1.0\%. The chirality flag is load-bearing
rather than decorative: the descriptor union is completely stereo-blind, with zero of RDKit's
217 and zero of Mordred's 1{,}613 descriptors changing value across any enantiomer or E/Z pair,
so fingerprint and descriptors are non-overlapping on this axis by construction.

\subsection{Implementation departures}

The specification is the reference source code --- not its documentation, and not always its
data files. Three upstream defects are reproduced rather than repaired, because a descriptor
that silently disagrees with the library everyone else uses is worse than one that is
consistently odd. Five E-state SMARTS rows are written \texttt{[N,nD2H0]} without a semicolon,
parsing as \texttt{N} \emph{or} (\texttt{n} and \texttt{D2} and \texttt{H0}), so the aliphatic
alternative carries no degree or hydrogen constraint at all, while the neighbouring carbon rows
use \texttt{[C,c;D2H0]} and do not. \texttt{[SeD2H0]} and its equivalents for Li, Be, Si, Ge,
As, Sn and Pb parse to element-\emph{number} queries with no aromaticity constraint, so
\texttt{aaSe} does fire on selenophene's aromatic selenium. RDKit's shipped
\texttt{Data/Crippen.txt} is stale relative to the table compiled into the library: two of its
110 rows negate by element rather than by aliphatic symbol, which is why an explicit hydrogen on
an aromatic nitrogen types as H3. We implement the compiled behaviour and pin it with
behavioural probes, so a future release fixing the text file leaves the port unaffected.

The test for whether to reproduce a quirk is whether the upstream quantity is a function of the
molecule at all. Two are not, and we diverge by design: Mordred's \texttt{InformationContent}
mutates a visited set while iterating over it, leaving roughly 20\% of molecules with an
atom-equivalence code that depends on input atom ordering, and
\texttt{ExtendedTopochemicalAtom} scores a pyrrole nitrogen 0 where a pyridine nitrogen scores
2, with the outcome turning on atom numbering.

Two descriptors are both named \texttt{BalabanJ} and are different quantities --- RDKit's uses a
bond-order-weighted distance matrix, Mordred's passes RDKit's own formula an unweighted one. On
naphthalene they are 2.888052 and 1.925368, and they agree exactly on molecules with only single
bonds, so a spot check on saturated structures would miss it; both are emitted under distinct
names. RDKit's $\chi$ is internally inconsistent in the same way: \texttt{Chi0n} and
\texttt{Chi1n} count all atoms and bonds while \texttt{Chi0v} and \texttt{Chi1v} are heavy-atom
only, so \texttt{Chi0n}\,$-$\,\texttt{Chi0v} is exactly the explicit-hydrogen count, and its
path finder counts rings as paths by documented design. We match RDKit throughout, which makes
explicit hydrogens value-changing rather than cosmetic ($d_3$-ethanol has
$\chi_{2n} = 0.158114$ against ethanol's $0.316228$) and is why no hydrogen stripping is applied.

Crippen and E-state typing dominated cost, being a generic subgraph search answering a question
that for most rows concerns an atom and its immediate neighbours. Both are replaced by a
dispatch on element and aromaticity plus a per-row predicate, with branch specifications
resolved by bounded backtracking as a system of distinct representatives rather than a tally of
bond types --- SMARTS maps distinct query atoms to distinct molecule atoms, and the two readings
diverge as soon as two branch specifications overlap. Multi-typing is preserved, since an atom
legitimately receives a tuple of type names rather than a best match. Crippen falls from roughly
78 to 1.53~$\mu$s/molecule and E-state from 636 to 0.834~$\mu$s, bit-exactly.

Two numerical kernels are written out rather than called from BLAS/LAPACK. Identical source
measured 138.09 against 218.93~$\mu$s/molecule depending only on which BLAS the host supplied,
and a released package cannot have its values decided by the machine it was built on. BCUT2D's
extremal eigenvalues use Householder tridiagonalization then a Pal--Walker--Kahan QL/QR sweep,
precisely the stages \texttt{dsytd2} and \texttt{dsterf} perform and in the same order, so
agreement is structural rather than tuned: maximum deviation $4.26 \times 10^{-13}$ over all
395{,}620 Burden matrices, and faster than the vendor library at 27 heavy atoms, where a tuned
library is mostly wrapper. The resistance solve uses reference LU with partial pivoting rather
than the shorter, better-conditioned Cholesky that $L + J/k$ admits, because the reference is
\texttt{numpy.linalg.inv} --- itself LU with partial pivoting --- and matching the algorithm is
what makes the arithmetic identical rather than merely close. Bit-identity is the binding
constraint: resistance bin edges sit at $0.1$, $0.5$, $1.0$ and $2.0$ and real values land
exactly on them (on tetra-\emph{tert}-butyl tetrahedrane every atom pair has $\Omega = 0.5$), so
one ulp moves a pair between bins and takes four further columns with it. Values within
$10^{-9}$ of an edge are snapped before binning, after which all three solvers agree on all
98{,}905 molecules. This supersedes our earlier values for those columns, which were an artefact
of one vendor's summation order.

Sentinels are propagated rather than tidied away. RDKit writes $10^8$ for unreachable pairs and
\texttt{BalabanJ} sums those rows, so $J$ legitimately collapses on a disconnected molecule;
skipping unreachable pairs instead gives a finite, plausible and wrong answer on 16\% of an
adversarial corpus but on about one molecule of a drug-like one, which is how such a bug
survives.

Verification is per-decision rather than per-column, since a count such as \texttt{NsCH3} lets
two opposite mistypings cancel: we check the atom's type tuple and the Crippen row index over
2{,}868{,}290 and 2{,}869{,}048 atoms respectively, and 98{,}905 molecules for the block
columns, against RDKit 2025.9.2 and Mordred 1.2.0 on a corpus loaded with salts, mixtures,
cages, fullerenes, macrocycles and rare elements. Exactness is version-relative: the E-state and
Crippen data are byte-identical between RDKit 2025.9.2 and 2026.3.5 and we score 100\% against
both, yet 19 of 100{,}000 molecules receive different E-state types because furans fused into
large macrocycles stop being perceived as aromatic. The RDKit version is therefore part of the
released column specification.

\subsection{Novel descriptor blocks}

Pairwise sums $\sum_{ij} f(i,j)$ require quadratic interaction across 2048 hashed bits, ratios
require division, and cyclic patterns lie outside colour refinement entirely, since WL-1
distinguishes exactly the graphs that homomorphism counts \emph{from trees} distinguish. Only
the third is an expressivity limit in the theorem sense; the other two are conditioning.

\emph{Resistance}, 60 columns, is built on $\Delta_{ij} = d_{ij} - \Omega_{ij}$, where $\Omega$
is effective resistance from the Laplacian pseudo-inverse taken per connected component. On a
tree $\Omega = d$, so $\Delta \equiv 0$ and the block measures path multiplicity alone, unable
to proxy for size or atom count. Only the 30 $\Delta$-derived columns vanish on acyclic
molecules; the Kirchhoff and random-walk columns do not, since $K_f$ on a tree is the Wiener
index. \emph{Cycles}, 33 columns, counts simple cycles of length 3 to 8 and is not
\texttt{RingCount}: the SSSR is a basis for the cycle space, not a list of cycles, and cubane's
cycle space has dimension 5 while the molecule contains 16 distinct six-membered cycles.
\emph{Conjugation}, 24 columns, recovers $\pi$-system components by union-find over RDKit's
conjugated-bond flags; coverage of conjugated-system topology in both libraries is zero, and
anthracene and a C$_{14}$ polyene are both one system of 14 atoms separated only by shape
(linearity 0.54 against 1.00). \emph{Stereo}, 23 columns, combines signed CIP parities at odd
order, which flips under reflection and separates enantiomers, and even order, which is
mirror-invariant and separates diastereomers; it is conditioning rather than new information,
since the fingerprint already carries stereo.

One trap is worth recording: \emph{a cycle is not its vertex set}. In $K_4$ three distinct
4-cycles share one vertex set, so de-duplicating enumerated cycles on their sorted vertex tuple
merges two of the three, which on a tetrahedrane derivative removed exactly $0.125$ from both
$\chi_{4n}$ and $\chi_{4v}$ (8.787913 against RDKit's 8.912913).

\subsection{Prediction head}

Hyperparameters are fixed and conservative, and evaluation is 5-fold Bemis--Murcko scaffold
cross-validation, never random. Folds are computed once per dataset and shared by every arm, so
fold difficulty cancels in the paired differences; results aggregate by mean rank and win-rate
rather than by averaging raw RMSE across datasets whose scales differ several-fold. Missing
values are passed in unimputed, NaN being a first-class input to XGBoost.

The one parameter worth tuning is \texttt{feature\_weights}. \texttt{colsample\_by*} samples
columns uniformly, which is the wrong prior for a matrix that is half sparse fingerprint bits
and half dense descriptors: a descriptor is informative on nearly every molecule while a given
fingerprint bit is set in roughly 2\% of them, so descriptors win the sampling lottery on
availability rather than on merit. A weight $w$ on the 2048 fingerprint columns against $1$ on
the descriptors turns this into a continuous knob. It does nothing unless
\texttt{colsample\_by*} $< 1$, and must act through \texttt{colsample\_bynode} rather than
\texttt{colsample\_bytree}, since weights apply at each sampling event.

The default $w = 1$ is dominated on both suites, and the curves differ in shape: potency
improves monotonically to $w = 100$, where the reweighted model beats a pure fingerprint, while
the physicochemical suite has an interior optimum near $w \approx 10$. The sweep was run to test
the hypothesis that descriptors cannot resolve activity cliffs, and that prediction failed:
moving from $w = 1$ to $w = 100$ improves non-cliff RMSE by 5.4\% and cliff RMSE by only 3.3\%,
so the gain is larger on the molecules that are \emph{not} cliffs. The surviving explanation is
a task-family effect --- MoleculeACE datasets are congeneric series, so descriptors vary little
across the whole dataset rather than merely across cliff pairs.
