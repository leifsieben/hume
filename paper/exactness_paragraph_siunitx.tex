On a diverse corpus of \num{42000} molecules (\num{41992} parsed; MW \numrange{9}{2209},
median \num{274}; \numrange{1}{163} heavy atoms, median \num{19}; \num{51} elements) we
find that \num{162} of \num{186} descriptors shared with RDKit and \num{412} of \num{968}
shared with Mordred are bit-identical on every molecule, and that \qty{99.99}{\percent}
and \qty{99.23}{\percent} of all cells agree to within \num{e-9} relative. The differences
are concentrated in four places: \num{114} molecules (\qty{0.271}{\percent}), all
organometallics and metal salts, where \texttt{HallKierAlpha}'s element table has no entry
and \texttt{Kappa1--3} and \texttt{Phi} follow it; the centred autocorrelations, where a
mean subtraction costs the bit-identity the uncentred columns keep; the information-content
indices at orders \numrange{1}{5}, where we diverge from Mordred deliberately because its
atom-equivalence refinement is numbering-dependent and we claim determinism
instead---order~\num{0}, the declared control, is bit-identical on all \num{41992}; and
\texttt{BalabanJ}, which is not our divergence at all, since RDKit and Mordred agree with
each other on only \qty{8.24}{\percent} of molecules and we match RDKit on
\qty{100.00}{\percent}.
