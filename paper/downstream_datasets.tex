\subsection{Downstream datasets}

We evaluate on 26 datasets, grouped into four families that are reported separately because they
are scored in different units. \textbf{Physicochemical regression} (RMSE): AqSolDB, ESOL,
Lipophilicity, and the PharmaBench LogD and water-solubility sets, plus the Photoswitch dataset.
\textbf{ADME and toxicity regression} (RMSE): the PharmaBench CYP2C9, CYP2D6, CYP3A4, human,
mouse and rat microsomal clearance, and plasma-protein-binding sets, together with CycPeptMPDB
PAMPA permeability, LD$_{50}$ (Zhu), and the Lombardo volume-of-distribution set.
\textbf{Binary classification} (AUROC): Ames mutagenicity and the PharmaBench Ames and
blood--brain-barrier sets, CYP2D6 inhibition, oral bioavailability, and human intestinal
absorption. \textbf{Quantum energies} (RMSE): QM8, QM9 (two targets: atomisation energy and
HOMO--LUMO gap), and QMugs HOMO--LUMO gap. Each dataset is split into five scaffold folds
(seed 0), so performance reflects information that transfers to an unseen scaffold, and sets
larger than 50{,}000 molecules are subsampled to that size. Every embedding is fitted with the
same deliberately untuned XGBoost head, so that differences are attributable to the
representation rather than to the head; the sole exception is \texttt{feature\_weights}, which
sets how strongly column sampling favours fingerprint bits over descriptors and is tuned in an
inner loop on the training folds only. Raw scores are never averaged across datasets, since RMSE
on solubility and on clearance are not commensurable: each dataset is expressed as a ratio to the
ECFP4$+$descriptors anchor on that same dataset and split, and these ratios are averaged within a
family, with the standard error taken across datasets.
